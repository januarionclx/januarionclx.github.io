I work with living systems.

Software that ships, organizations that run themselves, and — increasingly — the land and food they all depend on.

A position

I've written software for over twenty years. The longer I do it, the clearer it gets: the hard problems were never in the code. They live in the human system around it — how decisions get made, who is allowed to act, what happens when something breaks.

Frederic Laloux calls the next stage of organization Teal: the company not as a machine to be operated, but as a living organism that senses and evolves. Self-management instead of hierarchy for its own sake. Wholeness instead of the professional mask. Purpose that is listened to, not imposed.

So that's the practice now, in three layers: systems made of code, systems made of people, systems made of soil. Different materials, same discipline — clear interfaces, honest feedback loops, and the humility to let the thing grow.

The work

01

Software

Full-stack development, AI products and workflow automation — from game servers written in Lua and C++ at fifteen to LLM-powered products today. The commercial side lives at The Unit Organization, my development & consulting firm: performant, scalable systems for clients who need things done well.

02

Organizations

Organizational design grounded in self-management — Teal practices, Holacracy, governance and meeting structures that put authority where the information is. I help founders and teams move from org-chart-as-machine to organization-as-organism: explicit roles, distributed decision processes, and tension-driven evolution instead of a boss in every loop.

03

Ground

The literal living systems. I'm an investor in a vegetarian restaurant, and exploring agricultural projects in Venezuela, Madeira and elsewhere in Europe — early, patient bets on food and land. This layer keeps the other two honest.
